A Civilization of 7,000 Years

Where Every Dish Tells a Story

Iran — ancient Persia — is one of the world's oldest civilizations, a land where empires rose and fell, where poets wrote verses that still echo today, and where food became an art form passed down through generations.

"The food of a people is the memory of their land."

From the snow-capped Alborz Mountains to the warm shores of the Persian Gulf, Iran's geography shaped not only its people but every spice, herb, and flame that defines Persian cuisine.

Through the Ages

A Brief History of Persia

550 BC
The Achaemenid Empire
Cyrus the Great founded the first Persian Empire — the largest the ancient world had ever seen. Spanning from Greece to India, it united dozens of nations under one culture. The royal kitchens of Persepolis served feasts that would shape Persian cuisine for millennia.
224 AD
The Sassanid Dynasty
The Sassanid era saw Persian art, architecture, and cuisine reach new heights. Saffron, pomegranate, and dried fruits became staples of royal cooking. The tradition of slow-cooked stews — khoresh — was born in this golden age.
800 AD
The Islamic Golden Age
Persian scholars, poets and chefs led the Islamic world's golden age. Rumi, Hafez, and Ferdowsi penned immortal verses while Persian kitchens perfected the art of combining sweet and savory — a hallmark of Persian food to this day.
1500s
The Safavid Golden Era
Under the Safavid dynasty, Isfahan became one of the most beautiful cities on earth. Persian gardens, tile work, and bazaars flourished. The koobideh kebab and chelokabab became national dishes served from royal courts to street vendors.
Today
A Living Legacy
Iran's 85 million people carry a civilization's worth of culture, language, and food traditions. Persian cuisine — rich with saffron, fresh herbs, and slow-cooked flavors — is now celebrated worldwide. At City Kebab, we bring that legacy to Toronto.
The Land Itself

Iran's Breathtaking Nature

The Mountains
Alborz & Zagros
Snow-capped mountain ranges that give Iran its fresh herbs, spring water, and cool highland climates where pomegranates and walnuts thrive.
The Desert
Dasht-e Kavir
Iran's vast salt deserts, where caravans once crossed the Silk Road. The dry heat gave birth to slow-cooking traditions and preserved spices that define Persian flavor.
The Sea
Caspian & Persian Gulf
Iran is bordered by two seas. Lush green forests, rice paddies and fresh seafood define the northern coast — the birthplace of Persian rice dishes.
From Land to Table

How Iran Shaped Persian Food

Persian cuisine is one of the oldest and most complex in the world — a direct reflection of Iran's diverse geography, ancient trade routes, and rich cultural history.

Saffron — The Red Gold Iran produces over 90% of the world's saffron, grown in the Khorasan region. This precious spice colors and perfumes everything from rice to desserts.
Fresh Herbs — The Heart of Every Dish Iran's lush northern forests gave birth to a cuisine obsessed with fresh herbs. No Persian table is complete without sabzi khordan — a plate of fresh herbs beside every meal.
Open Flame — The Ancient Grill Kebab-making over open charcoal fire is a 3,000-year-old Persian tradition. The word "kebab" itself is Persian — nothing has changed since ancient Persia.
Sweet & Sour — A Persian Balance Pomegranate molasses, dried limes, tamarind and barberries give Persian dishes a sweet-sour depth found nowhere else in the world.
